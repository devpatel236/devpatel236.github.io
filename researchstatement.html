Research Statement

“The availability of restriction endonucleases is now such that any DNA molecule, obtainable, from a phage, virus, or plasmid, can be sequenced”

- Walter Gilbert, from "A New Method for Sequencing DNA"

Adeno-Associated Viruses (AAV) have become the primary vector for gene therapy delivery, requiring a deeper understanding of the virus’s biology to advance its effectiveness and safety. Consequently, current work in this field investigates the role of AAV capsid proteins, proteins that comprise the virus's protective outer shell, in configuring viral tropism, or the specificity of a virus to infect a desired cell. To this end, my research interest focuses on understanding the mechanisms by which the structure of these capsid proteins enables their ability to bind to cellular receptors on target cells and initiate the internalization of the viral vector. Uncovering and harnessing these mechanisms will allow for the design of more versatile AAV vectors that can target a larger variety of cells, thereby improving the effectiveness of AAV-mediated gene therapy delivery.
