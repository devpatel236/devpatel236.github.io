Research Statement

“The availability of restriction endonucleases is now such that any DNA molecule, obtainable, from a phage, virus, or plasmid, can be sequenced”

- Walter Gilbert, from "A New Method for Sequencing DNA"

Adeno-Associated Viruses (AAV) have become the primary vector for gene therapy delivery, requiring a deeper understanding of the virus’s biology to advance its effectiveness and safety. In this regard, current work in this field investigates the role of the AAV capsid proteins (VP1, VP2, and VP3) in modulating viral tropism. To this end, my research interest focuses on understanding the mechanism by which the VP1 protein, an internal capsid protein lacking receptor-binding accessibility, alters neuronal transgene expression. Uncovering and harnessing this mechanism will allow for the design of more versatile AAV vectors, enhancing the effectiveness and scalability of AAV-mediated gene therapy.
